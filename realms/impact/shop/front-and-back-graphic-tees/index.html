Front and back graphic tees

A front and back graphic tee lets the shirt do two jobs. The front is what people see across the table, so it stays small. The back is what the whole room sees when you walk away, so that is where the big artwork goes. Every Impact Threads tee is printed this way.

What is on the front

A small hand-drawn IMPACT script, high on the chest. It is the same on all four tees, apart from Bibi x NYC, where the script sits over a thin line of city skyline. It is quiet on purpose: you can wear it to work and nobody has to know what is on your back until you turn around.

What is on the back

A wide illustration across the upper back. Each one is a scene with a one-line tagline that lives on the site, not on the shirt:

  • Beware Killer Drones: kids flying a drone like a kite. Degeneracy of modern warfare.
  • Bibi x NYC: a giant Netanyahu in a suit walking through a ruined city. Coming to a town near you.
  • Ice x NYC: masked, armed agents over a person on the ground. Masked and unidentifiable.
  • Niger Delta Oil Pirates: masked gunmen on a boat. Degradation demanded dissidents.

Why the back

People read your back when they are behind you in line, on the train, at a show. That is a few seconds of attention, which is exactly how long it takes to notice a drone on a kite string and wonder what it is about. A message on the back gets read; a message on the front gets glanced at.
