Ice x NYC: an ICE shirt about masked agents

Most ICE shirts are text: a demand, an acronym, a hashtag. Ice x NYC, No. III in Impact Threads' Drop V·I, is a drawing of the thing people keep filming on their phones: immigration agents making arrests with their faces covered.

The print

On the back, in sepia, three masked men in body armour stand in the street with rifles. One of them stands over a person lying on the ground. Smoke rises behind a building in the distance. Their faces are covered and there are no names on them. On the front, a small IMPACT script on the chest and nothing else. The tagline: masked and unidentifiable.

The record

In 2025 NPR reported on federal immigration agents making arrests in masks and plain clothes, and on the fear and confusion it was causing. The Department of Homeland Security told NPR the agents cover their faces to protect themselves from doxing and threats, and did not provide evidence when asked. Senators Alex Padilla and Cory Booker introduced a bill to ban federal immigration agents from wearing masks during arrests (NPR). State lawmakers from Massachusetts, New York and Pennsylvania have pushed similar bills (Boston Globe).

What the shirt says

Nothing, out loud. It shows the scene and keeps the tagline on the site. The subject is an agency and how it operates, not the people it arrests and not immigrants as a group. The question it leaves on your back is about accountability: who is acting, and how would anyone know.

Why NYC

The name ties it to New York, where enforcement happens on the same blocks and trains as everyone's daily life. It is a city shirt the way a skate graphic is a local one.
