The UN report on Ogoniland

In 2011 the UN Environment Programme published an environmental assessment of Ogoniland, in the Niger Delta, requested by Nigeria's government. It found oil contamination there was widespread and severely affecting the environment after decades of oil operations (UNEP).

Oil spills in a Dutch court

In January 2021 the Court of Appeal in The Hague ruled that Shell's Nigerian subsidiary was liable for damage from pipeline leaks at Oruma and Goi, in a case brought by four Nigerian farmers and Friends of the Earth Netherlands (Milieudefensie). Shell said the leaks were caused by sabotage; under Nigerian law it had to prove that, and the court found it had not for those pipelines. The court also ordered a better leak-detection system on the Oruma pipeline (Court of Appeal of The Hague).

The Bille and Ogale cases in the UK

In February 2021 the UK Supreme Court ruled in Okpabi v Royal Dutch Shell that pollution claims by the Ogale and Bille communities could proceed against Shell's UK parent company in the English courts (UK Supreme Court judgment). That ruling decided where the case can be heard. It is not a finding that Shell is liable.

The OPL 245 corruption case

Italian prosecutors charged Shell, Eni and several current and former executives over the roughly $1.3 billion purchase of Nigerian oil block OPL 245, alleging that about $1.1 billion was paid out as bribes. In March 2021 a Milan court acquitted every defendant, including Shell and Eni (AP). In 2022 prosecutors dropped their appeal and the acquittals stood (VOA). On the corruption charge, the court record is an acquittal.

What the shirt says

Degradation demanded dissidents: the damage to the land is what produced the men on the boat. It points at an oil economy and the companies and governments running it, not at Nigerians or the people of the Delta, who live with the spills.
