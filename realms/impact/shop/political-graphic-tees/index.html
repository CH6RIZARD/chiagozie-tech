Political graphic tees

Most political t-shirts are a slogan in a big font. You read them in half a second and forget them in less. Impact Threads goes the other way: every tee carries a full illustration of something that is actually in the news, drawn like a skate graphic, with no slogan telling you what to think about it.

Four prints, four headlines

Beware Killer Drones is a pencil-style sketch of kids in a field flying a drone on a string like a kite, lettered "B.W.D." The tagline: degeneracy of modern warfare. Bibi x NYC shows a giant Benjamin Netanyahu, Israel's prime minister, in a suit striding through a wrecked city while people run below him: coming to a town near you. Ice x NYC is a sepia scene of masked, armed agents standing over someone on the ground: masked and unidentifiable. Niger Delta Oil Pirates is a painted scene of masked gunmen in a boat on the water: degradation demanded dissidents.

Why an image instead of a slogan

A slogan ends the conversation. A picture starts one. Someone behind you in line sees a drone on a kite string and asks what it is about, and now you are talking about it. That is the whole idea of the brand: fabric as the oldest form of protest, worn like streetwear instead of carried like a sign.

It also means the tee works on its own as a graphic. The back prints are detailed, illustrated scenes, the kind of thing you would hang on a wall, and the chest is just a small IMPACT script, so the front stays clean.

Who wears them

People who follow the news through memes and group chats, who grew up on skate decks and rap covers that were always about something. If you want a shirt that agrees with you loudly, there are plenty of those. If you want one that makes people look twice, these are it.
