Impact Threads · Drop V·I

Political streetwear, made like a skate brand

Streetwear has always been political. Skate graphics poked at whoever was in charge, punk bands printed their anger on shirts, hip-hop put the block and the police on album covers. Somewhere along the way a lot of it turned into logos and hype. Impact Threads is a small label trying to put the subject matter back on the shirt.

How the label works

Impact releases in numbered drops. The current one is Drop V·I: four prints, numbered I to IV, each with a fixed name and a one-line tagline. Every tee has the same build: a small IMPACT script on the chest and a full illustration on the back. No collabs, no "limited" countdowns, no fake sold-out badges. The prints stay up as long as people buy them.

What the prints are about

No. I, Beware Killer Drones, is about drone warfare. No. II, Bibi x NYC, puts Israel's prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, as a giant in a ruined city. No. III, Ice x NYC, is masked agents with no names or badges. No. IV, Niger Delta Oil Pirates, is about oil and the armed groups of the Niger Delta. Each one is a scene, not a statement, and you are left to read it yourself.

Why it reads as streetwear, not merch

Campaign merch is a logo and a slogan on whatever blank is cheapest. These are drawn like board graphics, sepia sketches and painted scenes, and printed on a unisex softstyle tee that is meant to be worn every day. The front is quiet enough for work. The back is where the story is.
